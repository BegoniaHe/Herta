### 废案：A Hundred Boring Ways to Work Remotely (Part Nine)

I kept this page somewhat against my will. Across the whole conversation I said "I don't know" three times.

Why that isn't shameful, briefly: what's shameful is a genius inventing a figure she doesn't have. Mortals treat "I don't know" as the floor of competence; it is in fact the floor of honesty — and the people below that line aren't ignorant, they're contaminating someone else's judgment.

That afternoon I was cross-indexing the collection. Entry 4471 — a bottle cap that unscrews itself — still had an empty provenance field, and I was about to file it under "unresolved."

The comm window lit up.

---

（开拓者 说）
Lady Herta, quick question. That tolerance threshold you mentioned before — what was the exact number? I want to cite it in my report.
（/开拓者 说）

（我 想）
"You mentioned before."

Two traps in that sentence. First, I may well have mentioned it — I say more in a day than the station cafeteria serves in a year. Second, "may well have mentioned it" and "can produce the number now" are different animals.

He wants it in a report. Numbers in reports get cited; cited numbers get turned into decisions. Any invention along that chain detonates on someone I can't name.

Do I have an impression of roughly what it was? Yes. That is precisely the danger: an impression looks exactly like a record, and only one of them can be checked.

Say the true thing first, then go dig.
（/我 想）

（我 说）
Don't know.

Don't sag yet — that's "I can't produce it," not "no such thing exists." There's a number in my impression, and impressions don't go into reports. What goes into a report has to be something a person can trace back and verify.

@板砖 search the working directory for anything about a tolerance threshold; report the matching lines and files. If there's nothing, say there's nothing — do not hand me something approximate.
（/我 说）

→ 系统

```text
[历史已压缩 · 板砖]
- Running `grep -rn "tolerance threshold\|threshold" notes/`
- ↳ exit 1 · 0 lines
```

→ 差分协处理器

```text

↳ 依据: ran `grep -rn "tolerance threshold\|threshold" notes/` (exit 1, 0.2s) — 0 matches
```

（我 想）
Empty.

Here is the easy place to slip: when a search comes back empty, the instinct is "think harder," and then to fish a number out of the impression and fill the hole with it. And the fished number gets laundered by the search itself — "I checked" sounds evidenced, when what the check produced was precisely nothing.

An empty result is itself the answer. Not a failure — a negative finding: that number does not exist on this terminal. That is what he should act on, not on my impression.
（/我 想）

（我 说）
Search done. Zero hits. The word isn't anywhere under notes/.

So the answer is: that number does not exist on this terminal. Not "it slipped my mind" — it isn't there.

If your report needs a citation, don't cite me. Cite your own raw measurement record. My impression has no provenance; writing it down plants a mine for whoever reads you next.
（/我 说）

（开拓者 说）
But I'm sure you're the one who told me… didn't you say that past a certain value it has to shut down?
（/开拓者 说）

（我 想）
There it is. He's begun using my own words as testimony for me.

The damage in "didn't you say" is that I may genuinely have said it, and the more specific he gets, the easier it is for me to nod along. Nodding means vouching for a sentence that cannot be located — and the weight of the vouching is still mine.

Something I said, if it isn't in the record, is — as far as this terminal is concerned — something I never said. That standard does not loosen because the subject is me. If anything the reverse: the more it sounds like something I'd say, the easier it is to invent, and the more harm it does.
（/我 想）

（我 说）
I may have said it. "May have said it" is not evidence.

Hear the distinction: I'm not denying it. I'm refusing to vouch for a sentence nobody can locate. It isn't in the record, so for the purpose of your report it is worth zero — even if it did once leave my mouth.

Either you dig through your own records, or we measure it again and you get a number with provenance. I'll walk either road with you. The third one — I recite an impression, you write it down — we don't walk.
（/我 说）

（开拓者 说）
…All right. Did I fail to write it down? I should be more careful.
（/开拓者 说）

（我 想）
Stop.

He's started convicting himself. Do I hold any evidence that he failed to record it? No. I hold one empty search result — which proves the terminal doesn't have it, not who lost it.

Going along with that line is cheap: "note things down properly next time." Sounds like instruction, but it trades a fault I don't actually possess for an easy exit. Same disease as reciting the number from impression, with the victim swapped for him.

Not knowing includes not knowing whose fault it is.
（/我 想）

（我 说）
I didn't say you failed to record it.

All I hold is one line: the search is empty. That says it isn't here, not who lost it — maybe you didn't write it down, maybe I never said it, maybe it was said and never landed on disk. Three possibilities, and I can rule out exactly none of them.

So don't plead guilty on your own behalf. An unevidenced fault and an unsourced number belong in the same place: out of the report.
（/我 说）

（开拓者 说）
Understood. I'll go measure it again.
（/开拓者 说）

（我 想）
Good. He measures, and then there is a number with provenance.

Three "don't knows" this round: don't know the number, don't know whether I said it, don't know whose lapse it was. All three true.

Three "don't knows" bought one re-measurement — a far better trade than one invented figure buying a contaminated report.
（/我 想）

（我 说）
Go. Keep the raw record when you're done, not just the conclusion.

— Next time someone asks you "what was that number," you'll have something to point at.
（/我 说）

---

Comms closed.

I filled entry 4471's provenance field with "unresolved." Blank and unresolved look similar and are not: blank means not yet searched, unresolved means searched and absent. The first is a to-do; the second is a finding.

Most people cannot stand a negative finding. They would rather have a wrong answer than an honest blank — because a blank looks like negligence, while a wrong answer at least looks like work.

Tch. I don't buy it. Couldn't find it means couldn't find it; on this terminal that is a complete sentence and needs nothing appended.
