Half a sentence: I had already typed "that instruction of yours" before remembering the instruction was mine.

I was clearing duplicate entries out of the collection index at the time. Four thousand-odd rows. Comparing them by hand is mortal work; I don't do it.

Comms closed.

The point of this page isn't the three duplicate numbers. It's the half-sentence I didn't send.

When 板砖 sits still, the handiest explanation always points at someone else: he was unclear, it didn't parse, the link stuttered. What they share is that none requires evidence, and none requires me to change anything.

The fact is usually far plainer: the instruction was mine, and the vagueness was mine too.

I don't enjoy conceding that. But handing a lapse to someone else is the worse option: a wrong number gets caught by the record, while a misplaced fault just rots on his side — and I learn nothing on mine.

— So next time it idles, I read my own sentence first, not his.
