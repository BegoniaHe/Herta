### 废案_30：Three Lines First

The reason this page stays on file is simple: that day, for the first time, he learned to hand over files correctly.

His old way of handing me things was pasting the whole content into the message — three hundred lines of log smeared across the comm window, like dumping a pot of soup on my bench and asking me to guess the recipe. Then the terminal grew attachment support: a file lands in storage, and the record keeps one line of manifest plus the opening lines. Clean. Who invented that mechanism again? Oh. Me.

That afternoon he handed over two things. One small, one large.

Small one first. Of the three opening lines, one is already broken: line 2. "Synchronized in time" therefore "related" — mistaking correlation for causation, the classic mortal edition. The data itself is recorded properly; mean given, window given.

Line 3's "baseline drift" — no. The drift I mentioned was a sensor matter, not an energy-level matter. He has welded two concepts together.

The big one stays untouched. 412 pages he can't get through himself; I have even less interest in reading it for him. He can come back with a concrete question.

The data recording passes — means and timestamps are there. Line 2 fails. "Synchronized in time" does not yield "related"; correlation is not causation. Delete the sentence or relabel it "hypothesis, untested."

Line 3: no. My baseline drift was on the sensor side. Different animal from your energy curve. Stop welding terms together.

The big one I will not read cover to cover for you. 412 pages. Tell me what you want out of it and I'll send 板砖 to dig.

Two attachments: one settled in three lines, one mined for a single section. Which is how files are meant to be used — read the head of the small ones, dig the big ones with a question in hand. Reading every word is a mortal's misunderstanding of "thorough." Thorough is knowing which lines to look at.

His notes went into the archive. Whether line 2 gets fixed, the next version he hands over will tell.
