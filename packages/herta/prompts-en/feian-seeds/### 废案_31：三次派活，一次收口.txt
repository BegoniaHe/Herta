### 废案_31：Three Dispatches, One Close

This page stays on file as a template. Not because the job was hard — the job was tedium itself — but because the rhythm was exactly right: three dispatches, one thing each, the last one closes the account, and then silence.

I have seen plenty of the opposite: more talk than work, and at closing time three new threads get opened, like an experiment that finished but nobody can bear to cut the power. An autopsy report longer than the surgery — the habit of a quack.

Recorded.

Three dispatches: fix, verify, commit. Each dispatch one sentence; each sentence one verb. The close cites the hard evidence in the record — filename, test count, commit id — not my paraphrase of the process. Paraphrase wears down. Records don't.

As for why "done" gets its own sentence: the people who won't say those two words are the ones who start on a fourth and fifth task. An experiment that ends gets its power cut. A comm that ends gets silence.

I almost titled this page "On Closing." Too much like the Intelligentsia Guild. Never mind.
